Why it matters

The danger is not only “the machine turns on”

Unexpected start-up

Someone hits a switch, a timer fires, or a control fails while your hands are inside.

Stored energy

Even a machine that is off can hold pressure, tension, or electrical charge.

Moving by gravity

A raised part can fall when support is removed, with no power at all.

The idea: lockout/tagout makes sure the machine cannot start and its stored energy is gone before anyone works on it.

Roles

Which one are you?

Authorized employee

Locks out or tags out machines to service or maintain them. This is the person who follows the six steps.

Affected employee

Operates or uses a machine that is locked out, or works in an area where lockout is happening. You must recognize a lock and never try to restart that machine.

Other employee

Works near areas where lockout may happen. You stay clear, and you never remove or ignore a lock or tag.

Energy

Find every source of energy

Electrical

Power lines, motors, and capacitors that hold a charge.

What to do

Open the disconnect. Then verify. Stored charge may remain.

Hydraulic

Pressurized fluid in lines and cylinders.

What to do

Close valves and bleed the pressure; block cylinders that could drift.

Pneumatic

Compressed air in lines and tanks.

What to do

Close the supply and vent the air to zero.

Mechanical

Springs under tension and spinning flywheels.

What to do

Release the tension or let rotation stop; block what could move.

Gravity

Raised or suspended parts.

What to do

Lower the part to rest or support it with blocking.

Thermal and chemical energy can matter too. The machine’s own procedure lists every source it has.

The procedure

Six steps, always in this order

1

Prepare

Know the machine, its energy sources and the hazards. Tell affected employees.

2

Shut down

Stop the machine the normal way, with its own controls.

3

Isolate

Open disconnects, close valves. Cut every energy source.

4

Lock and tag

Put your own lock and tag on each isolating device.

5

Release stored energy

Bleed, vent, block, lower. Get rid of what is left.

6

Verify

Try to start it. If nothing happens, you are clear. Return controls to off.

The procedure

Why the order matters

Isolate before you lock

A lock on one switch does nothing if another source still feeds the machine.

Release stored energy after locking

Once the sources are locked off, nothing can refill what you are about to release.

Verify last

Try the normal controls. The machine should not start. Then put the controls back to off.

Never skip step 6

A lock proves you intended to isolate it. Only the start test proves you did.

    The rules

    Locks and tags

    Your lock, your key

    Each authorized employee applies their own lock. The person who put it on takes it off.

    Locks stop, tags warn

    A lock holds the device off. A tag tells people why and who to ask.

    Do not remove another person’s lock
