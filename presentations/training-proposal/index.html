Summary

The case in one slide

The problem

Turnover is 28%. Exit interviews and engagement data point to first-line supervision as the main driver.

The proposal

A 12-week blended program for new supervisors, piloted with 24 people before any full rollout.

The return

At a 4-point turnover reduction, the program pays back in about 15 months and returns about 145% over three years.

The ask: approve a $76,500 pilot with a clear go / no-go gate at week 26, not the full $132,000 up front.

The problem

Why people leave: the supervisor gap

Promoted for skill, not leadership

Most first-line supervisors were top performers. Nobody taught them how to run a team.

Little feedback and no coaching

Exit interviews cite “my supervisor never told me how I was doing.”

Inconsistent handling of conflict

The same situation gets different answers on different shifts.

The evidence is suggestive, not proof

That is why the proposal is a pilot with a measured gate.

The solution

A 12-week program in four parts

1. Foundations

Role of the supervisor, expectations, communication. Weeks 1–3.

2. Everyday skills

Feedback, delegation, coaching conversations, with practice. Weeks 4–8.

3. Hard conversations

Conflict, attendance, performance. Role-play with scenarios. Weeks 9–11.

4. Apply and review

Action plan on the floor with a manager coach. Week 12.

Design principle: learn a skill, use it on the floor that week, report back. Short sessions on shift, not a three-day offsite.

The proof

How we will know it worked

Learning

Scenario assessment and manager observation of each skill on the floor.

Behavior

Team feedback pulse at weeks 0, 12 and 26. Share of employees with a documented coaching talk.

Results

Turnover and 90-day new-hire retention, pilot sites against comparison sites.

Gate target: a measurable improvement in 90-day retention and team feedback at pilot sites versus comparison sites. Turnover itself moves slowly, so it is tracked at 6 and 12 months.
