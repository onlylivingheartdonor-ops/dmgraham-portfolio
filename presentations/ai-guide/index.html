Know the tool

What AI does well and badly in learning design

Does well

  • Drafting and reorganizing content
  • Summarizing SME material
  • Drafting question banks
  • Spotting gaps between objectives and activities
  • Rewording for reading level

Does badly

  • Knowing your learners
  • Getting facts right without a check
  • Knowing your company’s current policies
  • Judging whether training changes behavior
AI drafts. People decide.

The workflow

From objectives to outcomes to assessments

1

Write a measurable objective.

2

Ask AI what the learner must do to show it.

3

Draft assessment items.

4

Ask AI to find objectives with no assessment, and activities with no objective.

5

A person reviews and decides.

Ask better

Prompts, before and after

Same request, very different result. Click a pair to see the “after”.

Before“Write a quiz about safety.”Click
After“You are an instructional designer. Write 5 multiple-choice questions for new warehouse hires on forklift pedestrian safety. Objective: identify three hazards when walking near a forklift. Use plain language at an 8th-grade reading level, make wrong answers plausible, and flag any fact you are not sure of.”
Changed: role, audience, objective, format, reading level, and a request to flag uncertainty.
Before“Review my course.”Click
After“Here are my 6 learning objectives and my 4 assessments. List every objective that has no matching assessment, and every assessment that matches no objective. Present it as a table and do not suggest new content yet.”
Changed: gives the inputs, names the specific task, sets the output format, and limits the scope.
Before“Make this simpler.”Click
After“Rewrite the paragraph below for new hires with no technical background, at a 7th-grade reading level. Keep every step in the same order, keep the safety warnings word for word, and list any term you changed.”
Changed: audience, reading level, constraints, and a list of changes so a person can check them.

Check everything

The review checklist for AI output

Six questions before anything reaches a learner. Click each one to tick it off.

Accurate?
Check facts and numbers against a source you trust.
Sourced?
Ask where a claim came from, then confirm the source really exists.
Right level for this learner?
Could your actual learner understand it without help?
Free of bias, right tone?
Look for stereotypes, hidden assumptions, and a tone that talks down.
Matches the objective?
Does it teach or test what you set out to teach or test?
Nothing sensitive?
No names, customer data or internal details went in or came out.

Know your limits

When to stop and ask a human

Some questions are not the AI’s to answer. Click each situation.

The content covers legal, compliance or safety rules

Go to the subject expert.A wrong answer here can hurt someone, so a specialist signs off.

The output could affect pay, hiring or other decisions about people

Go to HR or legal.These decisions need human judgment and a paper trail.

The question is about data, access or which tools are approved

Go to IT.They own the tools, permissions and security rules.

You are not sure the answer is accurate

Go to the SME.When in doubt, verify before it reaches a learner.
