Analysis

Training problem, or not?

Training can fix

Training

Supervisors and operators can recite steps but have never practiced verification on a live machine.

Training

Maintenance techs lack a clear mental model of which energy sources a given machine has.

Training

New hires on nights get the deck late, if at all.

Training cannot fix

Not training

Machine-specific procedures are out of date for 11 of 40 machines.

Not training

Lock stock runs short on second shift, so people skip locks.

Not training

Short-cutting is quietly rewarded when production runs behind.

Recommendation to the client: redesign the training and fix the procedures, lock supply and supervisor expectations. Training alone would not move the audit numbers.

Analysis

Three audiences, three needs

GroupCountWhat they must doDesign implication
Authorized38Apply locks and tags; verify zero energyHands-on practice, step by step, with verification
Affected140Recognize a lock and never restart the machineShort, scenario-based, 15 minutes
Other employees62Stay clear and never remove a lockOne-page card and a 5-minute briefing

A one-size-fits-all 45-minute deck over-trains the 62 and under-trains the 38. Splitting the audiences saves time where it is not needed and spends it where it is.

Design

Objectives that can be observed

Authorized
Given a machine with two energy sources, apply the six-step LOTO procedure in order with no critical errors, and verify zero energy before touching it.
Authorized
Identify every energy source on an unfamiliar machine from its energy map within 5 minutes.
Affected
Given a locked-out machine, state what the lock means and what to do if the machine is needed.
Other
Explain why a lock may be removed only by the person who applied it.

Every objective uses an action verb and a condition. “Understand LOTO” was the client’s original wording. I replaced it with something an assessor can watch.

Design

The strategy in four decisions

1. Practice before theory

Start each lesson with a short scenario where the learner decides, then teach the procedure that resolves it.

2. Tiered paths

Three tracks by role, so each group spends time only on what it will actually do.

3. Machine-specific, not generic

Authorized employees practice on a photo-based energy map of their own machines.

4. Assess the behavior

Replace the recall quiz with a scenario test and an observed skills check.

Assessment

Testing what people do, not what they remember

Before

  • 20 recall questions
  • “How many steps are there?”
  • Pass at 80%
  • No link to the job

After

  • 6 scenario questions, each with a realistic decision
  • Observed skills check using a 12-point checklist
  • Critical steps are pass/fail: no verification, no pass
  • Retake with a coach, not the same test
Why a critical-step rule: an average score can hide a fatal miss. Someone who gets 11 of 12 but skips verification has not passed.

Evaluation

Evaluation plan (Kirkpatrick)

1

Reaction

Two-question pulse after each session: “Was it useful on your machine?”

2

Learning

Scenario test and observed skills check, with first-attempt pass rate.

3

Behavior

Monthly supervisor observation of 5 LOTO events; compare to baseline.

4

Results

Audit findings, near-misses, and lock-supply stockouts at 6 and 12 months.

Levels 3 and 4 are the ones the client cares about, so I planned them first and designed backward from them.

Delivery

Risks I flagged up front

Procedures are out of date

Training on a wrong procedure is worse than none. Fix the 11 first or pilot on current ones only.

Production pressure

If supervisors reward shortcuts, nothing taught will hold. Needs a plant leadership decision.

Night shift access

Schedule sessions on all three shifts and track attendance by shift.

Assessor consistency

Train supervisors to use the checklist the same way, with a calibration session.

What this shows

How I approach a training request

Question the request

Separate training causes from non-training causes before building anything.

Design backward

Start from the behavior and results the client will measure, then plan the learning.

Test the doing

Assess behavior, protect critical steps, and measure the job, not the quiz.
